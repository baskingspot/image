# Float conversions, 2026-11-02

Decision 29 adds `rgb_f32` and `gray_f32` beside `rgba_f32`. They convert
through the general path's float pivot, as `rgba_f32` does. Float to 8 or
16 bits ran 190–250 MP/s on that path, which every caller of a float
decoder (JPEG XL, TIFF, PFM) who asks for bytes pays. This change adds one
SIMD kernel, `FloatAll` in `src/convert.zig`, for float samples to 8 or 16 bits
and back in the same layout (`rgba_f32` and `rgba8` or `rgba16`, both
premultiplied or both not; `rgb_f32` and `rgb8` or `rgb16`; `gray_f32` and
`gray8` or `gray16`). It runs 16 samples a vector: `@select` for NaN and
negatives, `@min` at 1, `@round` of the product, as `quantize` does; or the
integer over its maximum. The tail is one more vector ending at the last
sample when the buffers are disjoint, as in the other kernels, and
`quantize` per sample otherwise.

Output is bit-identical to the general path: "every SIMD path gives the
general path's bytes" converts random floats, with NaN, infinity, and
values outside 0 to 1, at every count and offset it uses for the other
kernels, and "float samples" checks rounding against values worked out
independently. The kernel uses no memory beyond its registers.

The general path's gray from float, `0.299 R + 0.587 G + 0.114 B`, did not
give back a gray sample exactly, since the three weights do not sum to 1
in `f32`: `gray_f32` to `gray_f32` through the general path differed from
the copy. Equal R, G, and B now give that value. This can move a float
gray pixel's 8-bit result by one where the product sat on a rounding edge.

## Method

`convert-f32-20261102/main.zig` times `Converter.run` on one call of 16,
166, 1024, and 8192 pixels (best of five batches), and over a 4-megapixel
image a row of 2048 at a time (64 MB of `rgba_f32`, past the cache).
`ab.sh` builds it against two copies of `convert.zig` and `pixel.zig`:
`before` is this branch without the two `fast` lines that pick `FloatAll`,
so the same pairs take the general path, and `after` is this branch. It
runs 10 interleaved rounds pinned to one core, then benchstat:

```
CORE=3 ab.sh OUT 10 BEFORE AFTER
```

Machine: Intel Xeon Processor @ 2.60GHz (4 vCPUs, AVX-512, a shared cloud
host), Linux 6.1, Zig 0.16.0 ReleaseFast for this CPU. Raw output:
`convert-f32-20261102/raw/` (`before.txt`, `after.txt`, `benchstat.txt`).

## Results

MP/s, median of 10 rounds (`raw/benchstat.txt`; every change p=0.000):

| pair               | 16 px, before → after | 1024 px            | 4 MP image        |
| ------------------ | --------------------- | ------------------ | ----------------- |
| rgba_f32 to rgba8  | 188 → 1309 (7.0×)     | 205 → 1855 (9.1×)  | 109 → 559 (5.1×)  |
| rgb_f32 to rgb8    | 226 → 1724 (7.6×)     | 247 → 2484 (10.1×) | 141 → 743 (5.3×)  |
| gray_f32 to gray8  | 405 → 2955 (7.3×)     | 472 → 7666 (16.2×) | 300 → 2597 (8.7×) |
| rgba_f32 to rgba16 | 188 → 1402 (7.5×)     | 206 → 1856 (9.0×)  | 108 → 497 (4.6×)  |
| rgba8 to rgba_f32  | 931 → 1257 (1.35×)    | 909 → 1257 (1.38×) | 424 → 535 (1.26×) |
| rgb8 to rgb_f32    | 472 → 1679 (3.6×)     | 441 → 1683 (3.8×)  | 340 → 744 (2.2×)  |
| rgba16 to rgba_f32 | 914 → 1263 (1.38×)    | 908 → 1256 (1.38×) | 418 → 475 (1.14×) |

Controls, pairs the change does not touch: `rgba8` to `bgra8` and
`rgba_f32` to `rgb_f32` are within noise at 1024 pixels and on the image.
At 16 pixels `rgba8` to `bgra8` read 10.9% slower (3005 → 2677 MP/s,
±11% in the after arm) and `rgba_f32` to `rgb_f32` 1.4% slower. The timed
loop calls `run` on a converter made once, so `fast`'s two extra checks are
outside it. The two binaries differ only in which kernels exist, so this
is likely code placement, not the change, but it was not shown.

Not tried: SIMD for float to float (adding or dropping alpha, gray to
color, color to gray). The general path runs those at 1.0–1.5 GP/s in the
cache and 380 MP/s on the 64 MB image (`rgba_f32` to `rgb_f32` above), so
they are closer to memory speed than the integer pairs were.
