# JPEG stage 2: 12-bit, lossless, and arithmetic decode, 2026-11-02

Wave 2, stage 2 of `image.jpeg.Decoder`: arithmetic coding (SOF9, SOF10),
12-bit precision, and lossless (SOF3). Two measurements: an A/B of the
12-bit output stage and lossless scan changes, and `just bench-compare`
against libjpeg-turbo, the bar and the oracle. docs/slop/jpeg.md has the
design; jpeg-20261102.md is stage 1's baseline and progressive results.

Machine: Intel Xeon Processor @ 2.60GHz (4 vCPUs, AVX-512, a shared cloud
host whose rows swing by ±10% between rounds), Linux 6.1, Zig 0.16.0
ReleaseFast for this CPU. Inputs: `bench/corpus-gen/jpeg/{arith,12bit,lossless}`,
44 files each written by libjpeg-turbo 3.2.0's cjpeg from the stage 1 sources
(bench/corpus/jpeg.sh): `-arithmetic` and `-precision 12` at quality 85 with
4:2:0 chroma, and `-lossless 1` (8 bits, first predictor, no subsampling).
Size classes: icons up to 64×64, others up to 1 megapixel, and larger (to 50
megapixels).

## A/B: the 12-bit output stage and the lossless scan

Profiling (perf) showed 66% of a 12-bit decode in the output stage, which
converted and scaled each pixel with a switch on the color space and two
divisions by the runtime sample maximum, and the lossless scan spending most
of its time on per-sample calls that copied the bit reader in and out and
checked for overrun after every value. The change:

- `Output.scaled` is specialized by color space and by the 12- and 16-bit
  precisions, so its divisions are by constants; 12-bit YCbCr goes through
  `sample.yccToRgb16`, 16 lanes of the same integer conversion and rounding.
- `huffman.difference` is inline, and the lossless scan decodes a row through
  a register copy of the reader, checking for overrun once a row (past the
  data's end the reader reads zeros, at most 32 bits a value, so a row cannot
  run away), with a path for scans whose components are all sampled 1×1.

Output is unchanged: identical to djpeg on the 46 generated conformance files
and the 12-bit and lossless bench files, and the unit test pins the vector
path to the scalar formula. Peak memory is unchanged; the change adds no
buffers.

Interleaved, 8 rounds of each binary alternately, pinned with `taskset -c 2`,
`BENCHTIME_MS=40`; geometric means of each row's median MP/s and of each
row's median ratio:
