# TIFF decode against libtiff, 2026-11-02

`image.tiff.Decoder` (wave 3, stage 1) against libtiff 4.7.2, the bar, and
the comparison arms Rust's `tiff` crate (through `image` 0.25.9) and Go's
`golang.org/x/image/tiff`. Every arm decodes a whole file from memory per
call into RGBA8 (bench/ops.tsv: `tiff-decode-none`, `-lzw`, `-deflate`,
`-packbits`, `-g4`); ours with a `Seeker.fixed` over the input, since the
inputs put the IFD after the strips. Throughput is megapixels per second;
a ratio is this package's throughput over the arm's (above 1, ours is
faster), the geometric mean over rows of each row's median paired ratio
over 10 rounds. docs/slop/tiff.md has the decoder's design.

Machine: Intel Xeon Processor @ 2.60GHz (4 vCPUs, AVX-512; a shared cloud
host, so only paired ratios are steady), Linux 6.1, Zig 0.16.0 ReleaseFast
for this CPU; C arms `cc -O3 -march=native`, Rust `target-cpu=native`, Go
`GOAMD64=v3`. Command: `just bench-compare 10 tiff-decode-none
tiff-decode-lzw tiff-decode-deflate tiff-decode-packbits tiff-decode-g4`
(exit 0). Raw output in `tiff-20261102/`: `summary.txt` (the table
bench-compare prints), `benchstat.txt`, `check.txt`, `raw-*.txt`,
`rss.txt`, and `rss-by-class.txt`.

Inputs, `bench/corpus-gen/tiff/` (bench/corpus/tiff.sh, checksums in
bench/corpus/tiff.sha256): every shared source, from 16×16 icons to the
50-megapixel mosaic, as 8-bit RGB that ImageMagick writes and libtiff's
`tiffcp` rewrites in strips of libtiff's default 8 KiB: uncompressed, LZW
and Deflate with the horizontal predictor, and PackBits (44 files each);
and the sources that are not photos thresholded to bilevel CCITT Group 4
(35 files). Every arm's output checksums the same as ours on every row,
but Go's on the G4 rows, which it inverts (Photometric is MinIsWhite and Go
inverts the decoded runs again); `tiff-decode-g4` is marked `own` for that.

The arms' own throughput, from those ratios (icons, to 1 MP, large):
libtiff 180, 2525, 2366 MP/s uncompressed; 73, 153, 135 LZW; 56, 183, 150
Deflate; 155, 1628, 1083 PackBits; 91, 1639, 1514 G4.

So: on icons we beat libtiff in every compression but LZW (0.90×), from
its per-file setup (TIFFClientOpen, directory parsing, buffer allocation);
uncompressed strips are 1.5–1.9× libtiff at every size, since rows go
straight from the input to the converter. Past icon size we are about half
libtiff's speed on LZW, Deflate, PackBits, and G4, while matching or
beating the `tiff` crate (but PackBits icons, 0.63×) and 1.9–17× Go.

Where the time goes is not measured yet (no `perf` in the orb). The
candidates, for the performance pass:

- LZW reads codes a byte at a time through `takeByte` and keeps the table
  in four arrays (prefix, suffix, first byte, length), so each code touches
  four cache lines; libtiff reads a word at a time, keeps each entry in one
  struct, and writes strings of length 1–3 and runs of one byte with fast
  paths.
- Every compressed byte is copied once more than in libtiff, from the
  caller's reader into the 4 KiB staging buffer (`Source`) that limits the
  codec to the strip.
- Deflate is `compress`'s inflate; libtiff here links libdeflate.
- PackBits and G4 spend per run what libtiff spends per run, but through
  `peekGreedy`/`takeByte` on the staging reader rather than raw pointers.

We hold the inputs and the RGBA output and little else: work memory is
the offsets and counts, a 4 KiB staging buffer, the codec (28 KiB for LZW,
`zlib.Reader.Buffer` for Deflate, 8 bytes per pixel of width for CCITT),
and one row: for the 8192-wide mosaic, in strips of one row, 104 KiB with
LZW, 140 KiB with Deflate, and 60 KiB uncompressed, half of it the 6144
strips' offsets and counts; 12 KiB for the 1024-wide G4 texture. The `tiff`
crate and Go decode the whole image to their own buffer before our harness
copies it, so they hold the image twice.
