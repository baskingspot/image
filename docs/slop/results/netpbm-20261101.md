# Netpbm decode and encode, 2026-11-01

`just bench-compare` for `image.netpbm` against Wuffs (raw PGM and PPM, the
bar), stb_image (raw PGM and PPM), Rust's `image` (every kind but PFM;
encodes 8-bit only), and `zune-ppm` (raw PGM, PPM, PAM, PFM; no PBM or plain
files). Throughput is megapixels per second. A ratio is this package's
throughput over the arm's (above 1, ours is faster): the geometric mean over
rows of each row's median paired ratio over 10 rounds. Before that, an
interleaved A/B of the decoder changes made while measuring.

Machine: Intel(R) Xeon(R) Processor @ 2.60GHz (4 vCPUs, AVX-512; a shared
cloud host), Linux 6.1, Zig 0.16.0 ReleaseFast for this CPU; C arms `cc -O3
-march=native`, Rust `target-cpu=native`. Buffers: input at a page boundary,
output 2048 bytes past one, for every arm; 50 ms a sample, every arm pinned
to the last core. The oracle and fuzz gates ran on other cores during the
runs, which costs every arm alike since rows are interleaved. Raw output:
`netpbm-20261101/compare/` (`config.txt`, `rss.txt`, `benchstat.txt`,
`summary.txt`, `rows.txt`) for every operation from the first run, and
`netpbm-20261101/compare-pbm-plain/` for the PBM and plain rows rerun after
the last two changes below.

The runs were on 98d1033 with this branch on top. The conversion rounding
change after it (938b0c4: 16-to-8-bit narrowing by the high byte, truncating
premultiplication) touches no pixel-format pair these rows use.

Inputs: `bench/corpus/netpbm.sh` converts the 43 shared sources (16×16
icons to the 50-megapixel mosaic; the CNN screenshot is left out because
zune-ppm fails it, see docs/slop/netpbm.md) with ImageMagick: raw PPM, PGM,
PBM, 16-bit PPM, RGBA PAM, plain PPM, and little-endian PFM, and raw pixels
for the encode rows. The mosaic is left out of the 16-bit, PAM, plain, and
PFM rows. Size classes: icons are at most 64×64 (13 rows), then up to a
megapixel (23), then larger (6 or 7).

## The A/B

The first build read headers through `peekByte` and `take` a byte at a
time, read native rows one row at a time, and converted spans of other
formats through a stack buffer. A byte read returns an error union, which
LLVM spilled and reloaded through the stack with a store-forwarding stall:
about 30 cycles a header byte, so a 16×16 PGM's 15-byte header cost twice
its raster. The change scans the reader's buffer for header numbers,
separators, PAM keywords, and plain samples (`skipWhile`, `number`,
`keyword`); reads a packed raster in one `readSliceAll`; and converts 8-bit
rows straight from the reader's buffer, the whole image as one row when the
destination has no padding. `ab.sh` timed the two builds on one core,
alternating, 8 rounds; `ab-benchstat.txt` has every row (geomean over rows
of the median MP/s):

| operation   | icons A → B | ×    | to 1 MP A → B | ×    | over 1 MP A → B | ×    |
| ----------- | ----------- | ---- | ------------- | ---- | --------------- | ---- |
| PPM         | 2765 → 7189 | 2.60 | 6699 → 7299   | 1.09 | 3354 → 3800     | 1.13 |
| PPM to RGBA | 1134 → 3897 | 3.44 | 3581 → 5069   | 1.42 | 2360 → 2830     | 1.20 |
| PGM         | 3153 → 8379 | 2.66 | 25292 → 36606 | 1.45 | 13136 → 14583   | 1.11 |
| PAM         | 939 → 3755  | 4.00 | 4619 → 4971   | 1.08 | 2679 → 3109     | 1.16 |
| PBM         | 1292 → 1381 | 1.07 | 2470 → 2367   | 0.96 | 2428 → 2332     | 0.96 |
| plain PPM   | 17.8 → 50.2 | 2.81 | 18.4 → 53.7   | 2.92 | 18.1 → 52.7     | 2.91 |

PBM's raster loop was untouched and lost 4% on larger images (p < 0.05 on
most rows), so two more changes followed, measured the same way against the
build above (`ab2-benchstat.txt`): PBM writes whole bytes as fixed 8-byte
stores instead of a variable-length copy, and plain samples of up to five
digits are found and valued 8 bytes at a time with SWAR arithmetic
(`digitRun`, `digitValue`) instead of a branch per digit. Six alternating
rounds each:

| operation | icons A → B | ×    | to 1 MP A → B | ×    | over 1 MP A → B | ×    |
| --------- | ----------- | ---- | ------------- | ---- | --------------- | ---- |
| PBM       | 1348 → 1489 | 1.10 | 2354 → 2689   | 1.14 | 2336 → 2702     | 1.16 |
| plain PPM | 45.3 → 48.8 | 1.08 | 48.9 → 52.1   | 1.07 | 48.1 → 53.1     | 1.10 |

`ab2-plain-benchstat.txt` also times PBM as a control, the same code in both
builds: within ±0.3% in every class. The SWAR parse loses 7% on the one
input made almost entirely of `0` samples (plant_01), where a branch per
digit predicts perfectly.

Against Wuffs, the bar for raw PGM and PPM: no row is slower (the
`<1` column of `summary.txt` is 0 for Wuffs on every operation). On icons
ours is 2.2–2.9× faster, on the rest 1.0–1.6×; the 50-megapixel mosaic is
1.9× for PPM and 2.0× for PGM, and 1.00× for PPM to RGBA, where both are
bound by memory. Wuffs narrows 16-bit samples to 8 bits and writes 16-bit
color only as BGRA, so its one 16-bit row is BGRA16, where ours is 4–6×
faster. stb_image (which also reads only raw PGM and PPM) is level on PGM and
PPM up to a megapixel and on large PGM, and behind everywhere else.

The rows where an arm is faster:

- **Plain PPM against image-rs, 0.90–0.96×.** A plain sample's position
  depends on the previous sample's length, so each sample costs a load,
  the digit test, and a count of trailing zeros in sequence, about 15 cycles
  however the digits are parsed. Lifting that chain (classifying 64 bytes
  at a time into digit and space masks and walking the sample starts with
  bit operations) is the next step; it was not done here.
- **16×16 PGM and PPM against image-rs (0.85–0.92×) and zune
  (0.79–0.83×).** From 32×32 up ours is level or ahead. image-rs writes into
  the caller's buffer as we do; zune's arm decodes into its own buffer and
  copies it out only while checking. At 16×16 the raster is 256 or 768
  bytes and per-call cost decides: the decoder spends about 210 cycles on
  the header and setup and 180 on the raster and the check for another
  image after it (rdtsc over 200000 calls). Neither Rust decoder looks past
  the image for another one.

All of ours is the harness's input and output buffers: the decoder
allocates nothing, holds 112 bytes of state, and uses about 4.5 KiB of stack
(a 4 KiB span buffer for the conversion paths and a 256-byte scaling
table); the encoder likewise. Wuffs is level. stb_image, image-rs, and zune
allocate the decoded image themselves, 1.5–2× ours on the large inputs. The
plain encode row is 770 MiB for both arms because the harness sizes its
output buffer at 13 bytes a pixel for the mosaic.
