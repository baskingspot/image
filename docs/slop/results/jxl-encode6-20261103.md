# JPEG XL lossless encode, stage 6, 2026-11-03

Stage 6 of `image.jxl.encode` (`wave5-jxl-encode6`): work tables sized by
the image rather than the effort, the last item of the "Next" list that
needed no owner decision. Method as in `jxl-encode4-20261103.md`.

| commit | change                                                               |
| ------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| tables | `workLen` sizes leaves, histograms, samples, and counts by the image |

Machine: Intel Xeon Processor @ 2.60GHz (4 vCPUs, AVX-512; a shared cloud
host), Linux 6.1, Zig 0.16.0 ReleaseFast; libjxl 0.12.0, one thread.

## What took the memory

`workLen` at 16×16 was 3.8, 5.6, and 8.1 MiB at fast, default, and best,
for files of 400 to 700 bytes. Its tables were sized by the effort, not
the image:

- the code's tables and the histograms, by leaves: the effort's limit
  or the decoder's for the image, whichever is smaller, where a 16×16
  icon in the corpus grows 3 to 7 leaves and its code 2 to 3 histograms
  (64×64: 17 to 57 leaves, 3 to 6 histograms);
- the samples, by the sample cap, which adds room for a palette's
  entries on top of the pixels;
- learning's counts, by 65 buckets for every predictor at every effort.

Larger images come closer to the limits: up to 951 leaves and 48
histograms at 512×512, and 1283 leaves (best) and 76 histograms for
kodim03.

## The change

- `maxLeaves`: also at most one leaf per 64 samples (at least 16).
- `maxClusters`: at most one histogram per 1024 samples, from 16 to 256;
  `Code.build` takes it as `max_clusters`, and `buildMemory` and the
  output bound count histograms by it rather than by leaves.
- `sampleRoom`: the samples' arrays hold at most four a pixel. The cap
  that steers sampling still adds the palette's room, so sampling makes
  the same choices; but a palette never needs that room (its channel is
  one sample a pixel, and `usePalette` keeps its entries to an eighth of
  the pixels, in at most four channels), so the arrays need not hold it.
- `learnMemory` and `learn`: counts for the effort's thresholds plus one
  buckets (17, 33, or 65) and the predictors a split is judged with (1,
  2, or 14), not 65 buckets for every predictor.

The caps sit above every tree and code the corpus makes at each size
(16×16: 16 leaves and 16 histograms against 3–7 and 2–3; 64×64: 256 and
16 against 17–57 and 3–6). An image that would need more gets a smaller
tree or code, still a valid file. On the corpus every file is byte for byte the same at every
effort (md5), and so is every bench row (`mem/checksums.txt`).

## Speed and peak RSS

A/B (`mem/summary.txt`), this build over main's, 3 rounds on core 3,
with libjxl at the matching efforts for scale:

| rows              | icons | ≤1 MP | >1 MP | vs cjxl (icons, ≤1 MP, >1 MP) |
| ----------------- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----------------------------- |
| fast (e3 rows)    | 1.00  | 1.00  | 0.98  | 0.57, 0.96, 0.78              |
| default (e7 rows) | 0.99  | 0.97  | 1.01  | 0.87, 1.12, 1.32              |
| best (e8 rows)    | 0.99  | 0.97  | 1.03  | 0.32, 0.79, 1.91              |

104 of 129 rows are within noise (±3–6% on this host); the 1 MP rows at
0.97 are inside their class's ±3–4%. The code a pixel runs is the
same; only where the bump allocator places the tables moved.

Peak RSS is the same (`mem/rss.txt`: 122.0, 125.8, and 143.0 MiB against
main's 122.1, 126.5, and 143.8), since the bench's process peaks on its
largest image and the dropped tables were never touched: a page counts
only once written. One 16×16, 64×64, and 256×256 file encoded alone
peaked within 16 KiB of main at each effort (`/usr/bin/time`, the
scratch encoder, best of 3: 16×16 1.3, 1.4, and 2.5 MiB at fast,
default, and best). What shrinks is what a caller must hand the encoder:
a caller that keeps one work buffer per thread, or encodes from a fixed
pool, needs a seventh of it for 16×16 icons at fast and default, and
under half at best.
