# JPEG XL decode speed: coefficients, reconstruction, and Modular, 2026-11-03

Changes to `image.jxl.Decoder` on `wave4-jxl-speed`, each measured against
the build before it, found by profiling `bench-image serve` with `perf
record` on the 1200×1200 photo of each operation:

1. **`decodeJpeg`'s coefficients** (`vardct.Jpeg.onBlock`). Each
   coefficient went through `try toI16(...)`, whose error union the
   compiler built on the stack as two 16-bit stores and read back as one
   32-bit load: a store-forwarding stall per coefficient, about 60% of
   the function's samples. The range is now checked once a block (a
   64-lane minimum and maximum; a scalar loop the compiler vectorizes at
   256 bits measured 9% slower), and the chroma-from-luma ratio of the
   quantization steps, a 64-bit division per chroma coefficient, is
   computed once per table.
2. **VarDCT reconstruction**:
   - `dct.llfFromLf`, the forward DCT that turns a multi-block transform's
     LF samples into its lowest frequencies, summed r·c terms with two
     `@cos` calls each for every one of its r·c outputs (a million `cos`
     calls for a 256×256 transform; 12.7% of a lossy decode). It is now
     separable, over a comptime table of the basis, still in f64.
   - A pass group zeroed each varblock's coefficients before decoding it
     (`memset`, 3·4·64 bytes a block); they are now kept zero, cleared
     after each block only where it was decoded (`decodeBlock` returns
     where its coefficients end in the order).
   - Gaborish is vectorized eight pixels at a time, in the same order of
     operations, and in a streamed frame it reads the group rows and writes
     the band's window directly, instead of copying the rows into the
     window and then into its ring.
   - The 16-bit output packing interleaves each pixel's channels once,
     instead of writing every channel at a stride.
   - sRGB's cube root (`color.cbrtV`) took three Newton steps with a
     division each; it now takes two division-free steps on the inverse
     cube root and one on the root (measured within 9.6e-8 relative over
     2.2 million inputs, against 9.4e-8 before).
3. **Modular and the entropy decoder**:
   - The weighted predictor's four error weights read `divlookup` in a
     form the compiler made a vector gather, about 20% of a lossless
     decode's samples on this CPU. The read is now volatile, which keeps
     it four scalar loads.
   - The tree walk takes two levels a step (`modular.walk`): a split's two
     children are adjacent, so their tests load alongside its own, which
     halves the chain of dependent loads. It needs no memory beyond the
     tree.
   - The hot loops (Modular's channel loop and VarDCT's coefficients) read
     symbols through an inlined `entropy.Reader.readInline`; an LZ77 match
     starts out of line.
   - Properties 2 to 14 are one `pub inline fn modular.pixelProps`, which
     the encoder can share.

Method: the `bench-image` binaries from `zig build bench -Doptimize=ReleaseFast`
of each build, run as `bench-image serve` side by side on the last core
(`taskset -c 3`), each row's loop sized once (`BENCHTIME_MS=50`), then 6
rounds alternating which build goes first per row, compared with benchstat
(median MP/s, p from a Mann-Whitney U test, n = 6). Peak RSS is GNU time's
maximum resident set of one `bench-image` pass over each size class's
inputs, as `just bench-compare` measures it: icons (up to 4096 pixels), to
1 MP, and over 1 MP. Operations: `jxl-jbrd` and `jxl-jbrd-progressive` (44
files each, `cjxl --lossless_jpeg=1` from cjpeg's baseline and progressive
JPEGs), `jxl-decode` (44 files, `cjxl -d 1`), and `jxl-decode-lossless` (44
files, `cjxl -d 0`), from `bench/corpus-gen/`, RGBA16 out, first frame.

Machine: Intel Xeon Processor @ 2.60GHz (Ice Lake, 4 vCPUs, AVX-512, a
shared cloud host), Linux 6.1, Zig 0.16.0. Base: origin/main ede392a.

Raw output in `jxl-speed-20261103/`: for each comparison (`c1-jbrd`,
`c1-jbrdp`, `c2-lossy`, `c3-lossless`, `c3-lossy`, `c3-jbrd`) the benchstat
table, each build's rows (`*-before.txt`, `*-after.txt`, benchstat's input),
and `*-rss.txt`.

## Results

before it; peak RSS of the whole operation, MiB, before → after.

| 1      | `jxl-jbrd` (44)             | 23.41  | 34.74 | +48.40% | 547.1 → 547.2 |
| 1      | `jxl-jbrd-progressive` (44) | 19.65  | 26.56 | +35.14% | 547.1 → 547.2 |

| 3      | `jxl-decode-lossless` (44)  | 4.294  | 4.986 | +16.13% | 989.3 → 989.3 |

Every row of every comparison is faster at p = 0.002 except four 16×16
rows (p = 0.02–0.08, +1.5–2%). Over the three changes, against ede392a:
lossy +32%, lossless +16%, baseline JPEG reconstruction +54%.

Notable rows. Change 1 grows with size, since it is per coefficient: 16×16
+1.5–1.9%, 64×64 +31–39%, the 1200×1200 photos +63% and +74% (30.0 → 49.0
MP/s), the mosaic +67% (30.7 → 51.4); progressive, the photos +46% and
+55%. Change 2: the mosaic +62% (17.1 → 27.8 MP/s), the photos +54% and
+48% (18.0 → 27.7), screenshots +35–54%, icons +2–6%, the 512×512 icons
(which have alpha, decoded by Modular) +12–21%. Change 3, lossless: 16×16
icons +22%, the photos +12% (3.86 → 4.34 MP/s), the mosaic +12% (3.91 →
4.39); its lossy and JPEG gains come from the inlined symbol reads in
VarDCT's coefficients (the mosaic +2.2% lossy, +4.1% rebuilding).

Peak RSS does not move beyond 0.3 MiB (the DCT's basis table is 49 KiB
of read-only data; the coefficients kept zero touch the pages they did
before).

Against libjxl, from its rows in `jxl-20261102.md` and `jxl-jbrd-20261102.md`
on this machine (not re-run): the 1200×1200 photo lossy at 28.7 MP/s
against libjxl's 37.7 (0.76×, from 0.44×), lossless at 4.34 against 4.9
(0.89×, from 0.76×), and the baseline JPEG mosaic rebuilt at 58.2 MP/s
against libjxl's 99.1 (0.59×, from 0.33×). Profiles
after the changes: lossy, coefficients and their entropy decoding 25%, the
XYB to sRGB transform 13%, EPF 12%, row copies 9%; JPEG reconstruction,
the `jbrd` writer (`jbrd.zig`) 29% and the coefficients 32%.

## Pixels

Changes 1 and 3 leave every output bit for bit as it was: their builds
checksum the same on every row of every operation (`ab.sh` compares them
before timing). Change 2's separable DCT sums in a different order, and
its sRGB cube root rounds differently in the last bit, so some samples
move by one 16-bit step. Against libjxl's RGBA16 output on the 43 lossy
bench files other than the mosaic, comparing ede392a with the final
build: 7 files are bit for bit the same; on the other 36, at most 0.02%
of samples change between the builds (apple.com: 4164 of 25.0 million),
each by one step. The largest difference from libjxl is the same on
every file (1 on most, 2 or 3 on nine), and the count of samples one step
from libjxl moves by under 0.4% either way (tecnick 001: 31559 → 31626 of
5.76 million; apple.com: 522532 → 524396; cnn.com: 146035 → 145867). This
is float rounding, within the lossy comparison's bound (peak 3, decision
45), and `just oracle` passes.
