# JPEG XL decode against libjxl and jxl-oxide, 2026-11-02

`image.jxl.Decoder` (stage 3: VarDCT and Modular, every feature the
conformance suite covers) against libjxl 0.12.0, the bar and the oracle,
and jxl-oxide 0.12.6, the other bar. Every arm decodes the first frame of a
file from memory per call into RGBA16 on one thread (bench/ops.tsv:
`jxl-decode` for `cjxl -d 1`, VarDCT; `jxl-decode-lossless` for `cjxl -d 0`,
Modular). libjxl runs `JxlDecoder` with no parallel runner, keeping the
orientation and not rendering spot colors (bench/c/libjxl-jxl.c); jxl-oxide
renders with `JxlThreadPool::none()` and widens its samples to RGBA16
(bench/rust/src/jxloxide_jxl.rs). On the lossless rows all three arms
checksum the same on all 44 rows. On the lossy rows each library's floats
differ within the format's freedom, so `jxl-decode` is marked `own` and all
44 rows differ from both arms, as expected. Throughput is megapixels per
second; a ratio is this package's throughput over the arm's (above 1, ours
is faster), the geometric mean over rows of each row's median paired ratio
over 10 rounds. docs/slop/jxl.md has the decoder's design.

Machine: Intel Xeon Processor @ 2.60GHz (4 vCPUs, AVX-512, a shared cloud
host), Linux 6.1, Zig 0.16.0 ReleaseFast for this CPU; C arms `cc -O3
-march=native`, Rust `target-cpu=native`. Inputs: `bench/corpus-gen/jxl/`,
44 files per operation written by `cjxl --num_threads=0` from the QOI
benchmark suite's icons, photos, screenshots, and textures, 16 and 32 pixel
icons, and the 50-megapixel mosaic (bench/corpus/jxl.sh). Command:
`just rust_arms="image-rs zune jxl-oxide" bench-compare 10 jxl-decode
jxl-decode-lossless` (the justfile's default `rust_arms` does not have
jxl-oxide yet, so `summary.txt` comes from `just rust_arms=... bench-summary`).
The measured build is the branch before four correctness fixes that
came after it (the native format fixed at `decodeConfig`, extra channels
upsampled on their own, the coefficient orders' permutation decoded in
O(n log n) instead of O(n²), and a work-bound overflow); of the timed
paths they change only the output packing of 8-bit lossless images and the
order decode of large transforms, and were not re-measured. Raw output in
`jxl-20261102/`: `summary.txt`, `rows.txt`, `benchstat.txt`,
`raw-*.txt`, `rss.txt`, `rss-by-class.txt`, and `config.txt`.

| lossy, over 1 MP (8)    | 13.7       | 0.48      | 1.37         |
| lossless, icons (13)    | 4.0        | 1.31      | 1.21         |
| lossless, to 1 MP (23)  | 4.5        | 0.77      | 1.08         |
| lossless, over 1 MP (8) | 4.2        | 0.75      | 1.08         |
| all (88)                | 6.6        | 0.80      | 2.34         |

We are faster than jxl-oxide everywhere but slower than libjxl on
everything past icons: about half its speed on VarDCT and three quarters on
Modular. On the 50-megapixel mosaic, lossy, we decode at 15.8 MP/s,
libjxl at 39.8 (0.40×), and jxl-oxide at 11.4 (1.38×); lossless, 3.8
against 5.0 (0.76×) and 3.5 (1.06×). On icons we win against both, because
our per-image setup is small: a 16×16 lossy icon takes us about 45 µs
against libjxl's 120 µs. jxl-oxide spends about 12 ms on every lossy file,
however small (0.02 MP/s on 16×16 icons); we have not profiled it, but it
looks like per-image table setup, and that is what its 66× on icons is.

The slowest lossy row against libjxl is screenshot_game-20XX_Menu_picture
(0.21×), the only file in the corpus whose color encoding has a gamma
transfer function (0.45455) rather than sRGB: XYB to RGB fell back to a
scalar `std.math.pow` per sample. The section below fixes it.

**Peak RSS** (GNU time, one pass over each size class's inputs: the inputs
and RGBA16 outputs every arm holds alike, plus the library's own memory),
MiB:

| lossy, to 1 MP      | 27.6  | 23.1   | 46.8      |
| lossy, over 1 MP    | 985.7 | 527.0  | 1852.5    |

The large class is the 50-megapixel mosaic: its RGBA16 output is 384 MiB
for every arm. Past that, libjxl needs about 130 MiB lossy and 25 MiB
lossless, since it renders group by group into the output; we need about
590 MiB either way, because we keep whole-frame planes: three f32 planes
for VarDCT (12 bytes a pixel), and the Modular channels as i32 for
lossless. jxl-oxide keeps whole frames too, and more of them. Below a
megapixel we are within 20% of libjxl and below jxl-oxide; on icons we use
a third of either.

## The gap, and what closes it

Speed: libjxl's VarDCT stages are hand-vectorized with Highway. Ours
vectorize dequantization, the IDCT's columns, EPF, the color transforms,
and the 16-bit output; the IDCT's rows, chroma from luma, Gaborish, and
upsampling are scalar, and we have not profiled this build to say how the
time splits between them and the coefficient decode. Modular's gap is the
per-pixel tree walk and predictors, where libjxl has more specialized
paths for common trees than our two (a single leaf, or the general walk).

Memory: rendering a single-frame image a group row at a time, straight
into the caller's buffer, instead of into whole-frame planes, needs two
group rows of planes (about 50 MiB at 8192 wide) plus the coefficients
when there is more than one pass. That holds when the TOC is not permuted
and no feature needs the whole frame (patches referencing the frame
itself, or a frame saved for later ones); the rest keep the current path.
docs/slop/jxl.md lists this as the next stage.

## After the bench: gamma transfer functions eight at a time

XYB to RGB now encodes every transfer function eight samples at a time
(`color.Tf.encodeV`): sRGB as before, and gamma, BT.709, and DCI through a
vector `powV`, 2^(e·log2 a) with a polynomial log2 and exp2, within 1e-6
relative of `std.math.pow` (tested against it, and NaN, infinities, and
values below the normals stay finite). Interleaved A/B, the two builds
alternating eight times over `jxl-decode`'s 44 rows, pinned to core 3, raw
output in `jxl-20261102/ab-before.txt` and `ab-after.txt`:

| row                                   | before MP/s | after MP/s  | change               |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------- | ----------- | -------------------- |
| screenshot_game-20XX_Menu_picture (γ) | 4.85 ± 2%   | 12.87 ± 10% | +166% (p=0.000, n=8) |
| photo_kodak-kodim01                   | 18.87 ± 8%  | 18.96 ± 6%  | ~ (p=0.900)          |
| photo_mosaic-50mp                     | 15.96 ± 9%  | 16.12 ± 26% | ~ (p=0.505)          |
| geomean, all 44 rows                  | 10.19       | 10.38       | +1.9%                |

The gamma file now decodes at 0.57× libjxl (22.7 MP/s in the bench), up
from 0.21×. The sRGB rows run the same code as before; benchstat finds four
of them changed by 1–6% in both directions at p < 0.05, which is noise on
this host, and their output is byte for byte the same (`bench-image check`
before and after). The gamma file's output is within 1.7e-5 of libjxl's
floats (clamped to [0, 1] as ours are), at most one 16-bit step; full
benchstat output in `ab-benchstat.txt`.
