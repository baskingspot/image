# JPEG XL decode speed, pass 3: output, copies, and the tree walk, 2026-11-03

Changes to `image.jxl.Decoder` on `wave4-jxl-speed3`, after
`jxl-speed2-20261103.md`, each measured against the build before it. They
came from `perf record` of `bench-image serve` on the 1200×1200 photo,
lossy and lossless. Every change keeps the output bit for bit.

Before them, a lossy decode spent about 22% of its time after
reconstruction: the XYB to sRGB transform as its own pass over the band's
planes (11.4%), a copy of each row into a chunk (2.2%), packing into the
native format (3.2%), and the shared converter's rgb16 to rgba16 step
(4.6%). A lossless decode spent 35% of its time walking the tree.

1. **The color transform and packing, done as rows are written.** A
   streamed band, and a whole frame that is only written (shown, not
   blended, not saved as a reference) with float planes whose rows are
   whole vectors, no longer takes its XYB or YCbCr transform as a pass
   over the planes: `Frame.writeRows` applies
   it to each chunk of 256 pixels, reading the planes and writing the
   chunk (`color.Xyb.applyTo`). Eight samples at a time, the chunk is the
   whole planes' bit for bit: such rows (VarDCT's are whole blocks wide)
   never reach the transform's scalar tail either way, and a frame with
   rows of another width keeps the pass. When the destination is the
   native format, or the native format with an opaque alpha added
   (rgb16 to rgba16, for example), `packPivot` writes the destination's
   rows itself and the converter is skipped.
2. **EPF's output, stored a vector at a time.** EPF wrote each eight
   pixels through `@memcpy` of a run-time length of at most eight, a call
   to `memcpy` per vector per channel (2.2% of a lossy decode). It now
   stores the vector whenever the row's stride has room, which it has
   everywhere but a Modular frame's last vector; the columns past the
   width are padding nothing reads.
3. **Large clears with the shared vector fill** (`src/fill.zig`, plan.md's
   memset item): `decodeJpeg`'s coefficient buffers, the multi-pass
   coefficients, subsampled planes, a pass group's coefficient block, EPF's
   ring, and Modular's channels, weighted-predictor rows, and single-value
   channels.
4. **Output pixels interleaved eight at a time.** Packing interleaved each
   pixel's three or four samples one at a time; two shuffles now pair
   the channels and a third interleaves the pairs.
5. **Modular trees walked flattened.** `walk` takes two levels a step, but
   its chain of dependent loads is still one a level: the split, then its
   children through the split's `child`. Each channel now flattens the
   tree it reaches (the static properties resolved, as `plan` resolves
   them) into entries holding a split, both its children's tests, and the
   index of its four grandchildren, which sit together (`flatten`,
   `walkFlat`): one load a step of two levels. A leaf child tests
   property 0 against the largest value, which always takes the leaf.
   A unit test compares the two walks on random trees with static splits
   and leaves at every depth. The flattened tree takes at most 1 + 4
   entries of 24 bytes per split; channels whose tree has more than 4096
   splits walk the nodes instead, so `Config.work_len` grows by a fixed
   393 KB (`modular.flat_bytes`). The lossless bench files' channels reach
   at most 530 splits (51 KB). Channels under 4096 pixels walk the nodes
   too: flattening every channel made a 16×16 lossless icon 6–10% slower
   (`e0-lossless`, and the first runs of `all-lossless`), and with the
   threshold it is unchanged.

Rejected: **sRGB's power as √(√a · ∛a) with a division-free cube root**
(two square roots and no division where there were three and one). It
measured +0.52% on lossy: the transform is bound by its 120 or so vector
operations per eight pixels, not by the divider. It also moved one 16-bit
step on about 0.1% of samples (13,768 of 12.5 million probes differ in
their 16-bit rounding), so it is not kept. A rational polynomial in √a
was fitted too (degree 4/4 to 9.8e-8 in f64) and dropped before timing:
in f32 its large coefficients lose to cancellation, 4.4e-7, four times
the current function's error.

Method: as in `jxl-speed2-20261103.md`: `bench-image serve` builds side by
side on the last core (`taskset -c 3`), loops sized once, 6 rounds
alternating which build goes first per row, benchstat (median MP/s,
Mann-Whitney U, n = 6), and GNU time's peak RSS per size class.
Operations: `jxl-decode` (44 files, `cjxl -d 1`), `jxl-decode-lossless`
(44 files, `cjxl -d 0`), and `jxl-jbrd` (44 files), RGBA16 out. Every
build checksums the same as main on every row of every operation, and
the 547 frames of the 298 JPEG XL files in `corpus/` and
`bench/corpus-gen/` hash the same.

Machine: Intel Xeon Processor @ 2.60GHz (Ice Lake, 4 vCPUs, AVX-512, a
shared cloud host), Linux 6.1, Zig 0.16.0. Base: main cb9422a. The host
was noisy during this pass: some lossless rows vary by ±20% within a run,
so their p-values are weaker than the medians suggest.

Raw output in `jxl-speed3-20261103/`: `a-*` to `e-*` are changes 1 to 5,
`e0-lossless` change 5 before the 4096-pixel threshold, `f-lossy` the
rejected sRGB change, and `all-*` main against the final build (`all-jbrd`
against the build before the threshold, which changes only small Modular
channels' walk).

Main against the final build: 19 of 44 lossy rows faster at p < 0.05 and
none slower, 21 of 44 lossless rows faster and none slower, 7 of 44 JPEG
rebuilds faster and none slower. A second run of each before the
threshold gave lossy +5.95% and +6.08%, lossless +9.37% and +8.04%.

Change 5 on lossless: the photos +7–14% (the mosaic 4.94 → 5.30 MP/s;
tecnick 4.75 → 5.40 before the threshold), the 512×512 icons up to +23%,
16×16 icons unchanged with the threshold; on lossy it is neutral, since
lossy's Modular streams are small. Main against the final build, lossy:
the 1200×1200 photo +12.9% (30.1 → 34.0 MP/s), the mosaic +11.1% (30.6 →
33.9), kodim01 +7.6%, 16×16 icons +8–9%; lossless: kodim01 +16.8%, the
photos +7–13% at weaker p.

Peak RSS moves by 0.2 MiB at most. `work_len` grows by 393 KB (change 5),
and `jpegWorkLen` with it.
