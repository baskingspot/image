# JPEG XL lossless encode, stage 7, 2026-11-03

Stage 7 of `image.jxl.encode` (`wave5-jxl-encode7`): decision 75. Default
learns its tree from `clamp(pixels × 4 / 16, 2^18, 2^20)` samples, one in
16 of the image's, where it took 2^18 at every size. Only images above a
megapixel (2^20 pixels) change; fast and best keep their counts. The
measurements behind the decision are in `jxl-encode4-20261103.md`
("apple.com: sample coverage"). Method as there.

Machine: Intel Xeon Processor @ 2.60GHz (4 vCPUs, AVX-512; a shared cloud
host, so only paired ratios are steady), Linux 6.1, Zig 0.16.0 ReleaseFast
for this CPU; libjxl 0.12.0, `cc -O3 -march=native`, one thread.

## The change

`Params` gains `samples_max`: 2^20 at default, and equal to `samples` at
fast (2^15) and best (2^19), so the clamp leaves those as they were.
`sampleCap` takes one in 16 of the image's samples between the two, and as
before never more than the image has. `sampleRoom`, `samplesMemory`, and
`learnMemory` follow from it.

## Speed and peak RSS

A/B (`samples/summary.txt`), this build over main's, default's rows (e7),
5 rounds on core 3:

| class | this over main               | this over cjxl e7 (main's) |
| ----- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| icons | 0.98 (13 of 13 within noise) | 0.86 (0.87)                |
| ≤1 MP | 1.00 (22 of 23)              | 1.14 (1.15)                |
| >1 MP | 0.91 (4 slower)              | 1.23 (1.34)                |

The icons and ≤1 MP rows run the same code on the same samples; their
ratios are noise (±3–5%). Above a megapixel the cost follows the samples:
apple.com 0.77 and cnn.com 0.83 (both reach 2^20), the 1200×1200 photos
0.94 and 0.91 (2^18 → 360,000), amazon.com, wikipedia, and plant_02 within
noise. The scratch encoder's corpus run, beside main's on another core,
agrees: 24.1 → 25.1 s for the corpus (+4.1%), 15.6 → 16.7 s above a
megapixel (+6.9%).

Peak RSS over the op (`samples/rss.txt`): 125.8 → 142.4 MiB (+16.6 MiB),
against libjxl's 260.5. The bench's process peaks on its largest image,
which now learns from 2^20 samples where it took 2^18; each sample keeps
a byte a property and a predictor (28 at default) plus a 4-byte index, so
the 786,432 more samples add 25 MB to `workLen`, most of it written:
