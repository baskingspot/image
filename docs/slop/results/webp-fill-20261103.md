# WebP lossy: the coefficient clear and the encoder's histograms on `fill`

`memset-20261103.md` found compiler_rt's byte-wise `memset` at 4.5% of
webp-decode-lossy and 4.9% of webp-decode-anim. In the decoder, that is
`vp8.decodeFrame`'s clear of a macroblock's coefficients
(`coef = @splat(@splat(0))`, 768 bytes after every macroblock that had
any, and once a frame), which LLVM lowers to a `memset` call. It now uses
`src/fill.zig`'s `fill`. The lossy encoder's `zeroHist`, which zeroed
its two 9 KiB token histograms with its own volatile vector stores, now
calls `fill` too.

The rest of webp-decode-anim's share (`webp.anim` 1.9% and
`PixelBuffer.row` 0.9% in the profile) is the bench's own disposal in
`bench/zig/webp.zig` (`@memset` of each disposed row), a caller's fill
like gif-decode-anim's; it is not changed here.

## A/B

CPU: Intel Xeon @ 2.60 GHz (4 threads, AVX-512), Zig 0.16.0 ReleaseFast
native. Main fa9c040 (A) against the branch (B), `bench-image bench OP`
with `BENCHTIME_MS=50`, eight rounds alternating A and B per op, pinned
to core 3 (`ab.sh`); benchstat on MP/s (`ab-benchstat.txt`, raw rows
`ab-A.txt` and `ab-B.txt`). Per op and class, the geomean of the rows'
median ratios B/A, and the rows benchstat calls faster or slower at
p < 0.05 (`ab-summary.txt`, from `absum.py`):

| op                     | rows | B/A   | icons | to 1 MP | over 1 MP | faster/slower |
| ---------------------- | ---: | ----- | ----- | ------- | --------- | ------------: |
| webp-decode-lossy      |   44 | 1.056 | 1.042 | 1.060   | 1.065     |          28/0 |
| webp-decode-anim       |    6 | 1.037 | 1.022 | 1.044   | -         |           1/0 |
| webp-encode-lossy      |   44 | 1.010 | 1.029 | 0.997   | 1.016     |           1/2 |
| webp-encode-lossy-fast |   44 | 1.005 | 1.012 | 1.004   | 0.996     |           0/0 |

- Lossy decode is 5.6% faster (2–12% per row), about the share the
  clear had. The animation op gains 3.7%: its lossy frames' clear.
- The encoder rows are unchanged within the 3% this binary moves when
  code moves (`memset-20261103.md`), so `zeroHist` uses the shared fill.

Memory: unchanged; the same bytes are written.
