# image.resize against stb_image_resize2 and libvips, 2026-11-05

`just bench-compare 10` over the nine resize rows (bench/ops.tsv), on one
core of an Intel Xeon @ 2.60GHz (4 threads, AVX-512), image in ReleaseFast
for the native CPU, the C arms at `cc -O3 -march=native`, 50 ms a sample,
10 interleaved rounds pinned with `taskset`, compared with benchstat. MP/s
are source megapixels a second. The arms:

- `stb`: stb_image_resize2 2.18, `stbir_resize_uint8_linear` (`_srgb` for
  the sRGB row), its defaults: Mitchell shrinking, Catmull-Rom growing,
  edges clamped. It allocates its working memory on every call.
- `libvips`: libvips 8.18.7 (nixpkgs), `vips_reduce` with Mitchell to
  shrink and `vips_resize` with Catmull-Rom (its bicubic) to grow, one
  thread, operation cache off, straight alpha premultiplied around it; no
  sRGB row (libvips filters stored values).

Pillow-SIMD was not wired: it is a Python extension whose resampler is
not a C library, and libvips was one `pkg-config` line from the binary
cache.

Inputs (bench/corpus/resize.sh): the icon suite's alpha (gray8) and
premultiplied BGRA at 16, 32, 64, 128, and 256 pixels square, 4000×3000
RGB photos (the 50-megapixel mosaic and a Wikipedia photo, resized), Kodak
photos at 512×384 and 1000×750 for doubling, and raw/rgba8 (16 to 4096
square) for straight alpha. "icon" is up to 64×64, "1mp" up to a
megapixel, "large" above.

Against stb_image_resize2: 0.97 over every row. The glyph rows monstar
makes (gray8 and premultiplied BGRA, 16 to 256 pixels) are 0.83–1.20,
faster than stb from 128 pixels up and slower at 16 and 32, where stb's
per-call cost is lower (gray 16→32: 2.1 µs against 2.7). The losses are
the photo thumbnails (0.74–0.75), RGB doubling (0.89), and the sRGB
thumbnail (0.83).

libvips is 7 to 258 times slower than both on everything but thumbnails,
where its pipeline setup (about 0.5 ms a call) does not matter and its
reduce is 1.6–2.2× faster than image. libvips's reduce filters 8-bit
samples with integer weights (its source, not read here, is the place to
confirm the details), and 16-bit integer lanes are twice as many as
`f32`'s. That is the direction for large 8-bit downscales; integer weights
would give up some of `f32`'s agreement with stb (within one step today),
so it is the owner's call.

## How it got there

The first version (summary-first.txt; its libvips arm called reduceh and
reducev without the line cache vips_reduce puts between them, about four
times slower than vips_reduce) ran at 0.28 of stb over every row, 0.16 on
gray 256→64. perf, on a microbench of `resize.resize` pinned to one core
(MP/s of the source, one run each, so differences under about 5% are
noise):

| Change                                                                                               | gray8 256→64 | gray8 128→256 | bgra8p 256→64 |      rgb8 4000→256 |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -----------: | ------------: | ------------: | -----------------: |
| first version: per-pixel dot product, `@reduce(.Add)` (an ordered chain of 15 adds), scalar tail     |          285 |            38 |           173 |                185 |
| weights padded to whole vectors and repeated per float; RGB as 4 floats; fold by halves              |          611 |           109 |           357 | 99 (unpack scalar) |
| groups of pixels summed side by side, one shuffle-tree fold, unrolled column sums, vector RGB unpack |          699 |           119 |           501 |                480 |
| widest window instead of the bound, vector pack paths, no division in the weights                    |          820 |           158 |           547 |                489 |
| vertical first for one float a pixel that shrinks (`v_first`)                                        |         1314 |           157 |           554 |                489 |
| short tap runs unrolled, cubic coefficients over 6 at comptime                                       |         1544 |           208 |           554 |                484 |
| weights computed for one period of the ratio and copied                                              |         2118 |           290 |           670 |                492 |
| square resizes take the vertical weights from the horizontal table                                   |         1986 |           291 |           683 |                496 |

`v_first` for four floats a pixel was measured and rejected: RGB
4000×3000→256 ran at 315 MP/s against 489 horizontal first, with the work
memory 6.5 times larger (4.5 MB against 0.7), since the vertical pass then
streams 64 source rows of 16,000 floats from beyond L2 for every
destination row. BGRA 256→64 did not change. For one float a pixel it is
a pure speed trade on work memory: gray8 64→16 takes 9 KiB rather than
5 KiB.

The weight tricks (periods, the square reuse) are what move the icon rows:
at 16 pixels, computing weights was a third of a call.
